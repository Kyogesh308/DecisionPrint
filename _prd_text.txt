Executive Summary 
DecisionPrint is an AI-powered organizational decision-memory system. It turns fragmented project artifacts into a 
living record of decisions, their reasons, constraints, alternatives, assumptions, outcomes, exceptions, and later 
consequences. Its purpose is not document search. Its purpose is to reconstruct how organizational reasoning evolved 
over time and to make that history useful at the next decision point. 
Hindsight is the product’s memory engine, not an accessory. Source documents and transcripts are retained into an 
organizational memory bank. Hindsight extracts facts, temporal information, entities, and relationships; its recall 
operation retrieves through semantic, keyword, graph, and temporal strategies; background observations consolidate 
repeated evidence into evolving beliefs; reflect performs deeper reasoning over remembered material; and mental 
models can maintain standing organizational summaries. DecisionPrint adds the smallest deterministic layer required 
for canonical Decision objects, source permissions, comparison of old versus current constraints, drift flags, and 
auditability. 
The hackathon MVP proves one loop exceptionally well: ingest historical project evidence; ingest a new project 
meeting; extract a decision and its context; retain that evidence in Hindsight; ask a new decision question; recall the 
relevant historical decision; compare historical and current constraints; surface Decision Drift; produce a source-
backed decision brief; then ingest a later outcome or incident and show that the organizational memory has changed. 
The demo must make one fact visually undeniable: without persistent longitudinal memory, “Kafka 
was rejected” looks like a rule; with Hindsight memory, it becomes “Kafka was rejected in 2025 
because there were only two consumers and no replay requirement — conditions that no longer 
match this project.” 
Problem Statement 
Organizations document outputs better than reasoning. PRDs record requirements, architecture documents record 
chosen designs, tickets record work, meeting notes record fragments, and postmortems record failures. The 
connective tissue — why an option was chosen, which assumptions were true then, what alternatives were rejected, 
and what happened later — is scattered across time and tools. 
This creates four recurring failures: institutional amnesia when people leave; cargo-cult reuse of old decisions after 
their constraints have changed; repeated exploration of already-tested alternatives; and weak organizational learning 
because downstream outcomes are not connected back to the decisions that preceded them. 
Generic retrieval systems reduce search cost but do not solve the temporal reasoning problem. A similarity search can 
find “Do not use Kafka,” yet still miss that the statement was contingent on two consumers, moderate traffic, and no 
replay. DecisionPrint treats a decision as a time-bound claim embedded in a changing world. 
Product Vision 
Create an organizational memory that can answer not merely “What do our documents say?” but “What did we believe, 
why did we believe it, what evidence changed, and what should be reconsidered now?” 
Product Thesis 
The most valuable organizational knowledge is often conditional and longitudinal. A technical choice is meaningful only 
in relation to its constraints; an organizational principle is trustworthy only when its supporting evidence is visible; and a 
past decision should influence a new one only to the extent that the contexts are comparable. 
Therefore, DecisionPrint treats persistent memory, temporal context, evidence consolidation, and evolving 
observations as first-class product primitives. Hindsight is uniquely central because the product’s core behavior 
depends on retain → evolving memory → recall/reflect rather than one-shot retrieval.

Product Principles 
1. Memory before generation. Retrieve organizational memory before allowing the answer model to reason about 
company-specific history. 
2. Conditional, not absolute. Store the circumstances around a decision so “rejected” never automatically means 
“forbidden forever.” 
3. Time is data. Preserve both when an event occurred and when the system learned it. 
4. Evidence survives synthesis. Every high-impact claim must lead back to retained evidence and source artifacts. 
5. Observations evolve; history remains inspectable. New evidence may refine an organizational belief but must not 
erase the underlying facts. 
6. Human authority stays explicit. Drift, causal links, capability gaps, and recommendations are prompts for review, 
not automatic policy changes. 
7. One memory engine. Do not introduce a second vector/graph memory stack merely to duplicate Hindsight.

Hindsight-Centric Memory Architecture 
DecisionPrint uses one organization memory bank for the hackathon dataset, with strict tags/metadata for project, 
source class, sensitivity, and lifecycle. This choice enables cross-project learning — essential to the product — while 
allowing recall to be scoped to authorized projects. A production deployment can partition banks by tenant/business 
boundary where isolation requirements demand it.

projection optimized for UI and deterministic comparisons. The app database must never become a shadow 
knowledge base containing all organizational memory.

Application metadata: stable IDs, normalized fields, status, supersession pointers, source locators, and explicit

Decision Lifecycle 
PROPOSED / DISCUSSED 
      ↓ evidence of commitment 
DECIDED 
      ↓ implementation / later events 
OBSERVED OUTCOME 
      ↓ new constraints or contradictory evidence 
RECONSIDERATION CANDIDATE 
      ├── no change → ACTIVE WITH NEW EVIDENCE 
      ├── scoped exception → EXCEPTION 
      └── changed decision → SUPERSEDED → NEW DECISION 
Decisions are append-only historical events. A superseding decision does not overwrite the original memory. The 
application updates lifecycle metadata while Hindsight retains both historical and newer evidence, enabling temporal 
recall and inspection of evolution. 
Decision Drift 
Decision Drift identifies when the premises supporting an older decision appear materially different from current 
evidence. It is a review signal, not an automated reversal. 
Drift triggers 
 
A historical constraint no longer holds.

 
Repeated exceptions accumulate against a broad rule. 
 
A previously rejected technology increasingly appears in successful projects. 
 
The same decision is repeatedly reopened. 
 
Operational evidence contradicts an assumption that supported the original decision. 
MVP drift algorithm 
8. Recall historical decision memories relevant to the current question and entities. 
9. Extract/normalize comparable constraints from the historical decision and current project context. 
10. Classify each pair as same, changed, newly present, missing/unknown, or incomparable. 
11. Compute an explainable drift score from material changed constraints, weighted by whether the historical reason 
explicitly depended on them. 
12. Ask Hindsight reflect to synthesize the organizational context using recalled evidence, but require the UI to show 
the deterministic constraint delta beside the synthesis. 
13. Label the result “reconsideration warranted” only when threshold conditions are met; never mutate the decision 
automatically. 
drift_score = weighted_changed_reason_constraints / weighted_known_reason_constraints 
 
Example: 
consumer_count: 2 → 15        CHANGED (material) 
replay_required: no → yes     CHANGED (material) 
traffic: moderate → high      CHANGED (material) 
team_size: small → unknown    UNKNOWN 
 
Signal: HIGH DRIFT · original premises materially differ

Hindsight Integration 
Bank strategy 
MVP: one organization bank to enable cross-project recall, with project and visibility tags. Production: one bank per 
tenant or hard isolation boundary; use tags only inside a boundary where cross-project learning is authorized. 
Hindsight banks are isolated memory stores, so bank boundaries should align with real disclosure boundaries rather 
than arbitrary UI organization. 
Retain 
Every source is retained with source context, project tags/metadata, and an event timestamp where known. The bank 
retain mission should explicitly prioritize technical/product decisions, alternatives, constraints, assumptions, reasons, 
outcomes, incidents, exceptions, and downstream consequences while ignoring meeting logistics. 
retain_mission: 
"Extract durable organizational decision memory. Preserve what was decided, why, 
constraints and assumptions at the time, alternatives considered/rejected, relevant 
technologies and stakeholders, dates, dependencies, outcomes, incidents, exceptions, 
and explicit causal claims. Ignore greetings, scheduling, and low-value chatter." 
Use verbose extraction for high-value decision/postmortem sources if hackathon latency permits; use concise for bulk 
background material. Store stable source IDs so recalled facts can be traced to their document/chunk associations. 
Recall 
For decision questions, recall across world, experience, and observation memories, preferring observations where 
supported while retaining access to unconsolidated recent facts. Apply project/visibility tag filters based on 
authorization. Request entity information and use temporal query context for “at the time” questions. 
Reflect 
Use reflect for questions requiring synthesis across multiple memories: recurring risks, why organizational behavior 
changed, whether evidence suggests a prior assumption is stale, or what prior outcomes matter. Do not use reflect as 
a substitute for the deterministic constraint comparator. The answer layer should receive the comparator output as 
additional context and return a structured brief. 
Observations 
Observations are crucial to “living memory.” They consolidate repeated evidence into deduplicated beliefs and are 
refined as supporting or contradictory evidence arrives. DecisionPrint uses them for patterns such as “small teams 
repeatedly avoid operationally heavy infrastructure” or “streaming requirements are increasingly common.” The UI 
labels these as OBSERVATION, not historical fact.

Mental models / knowledge pages 
Create only a few high-value standing summaries for the demo, for example “Current architecture principles,” 
“Recurring operational risks,” and “Technology adoption patterns.” Mental models are appropriate because they are

wiki-like view, but they are optional to the killer loop. 
Memory history and source association 
Use memory/document IDs and observation history where available to show that an organizational belief has 
accumulated or changed evidence. The UI does not need to expose Hindsight internals everywhere; it should expose 
the evidence count, supporting sources, and a simple “memory evolved” timeline.

 
Confidential information: store only in authorized tenant/bank boundaries; encrypt storage/transport in production.

Project isolation: authorization scope is resolved before recall; tag filters are derived server-side, never trusted

 
Sensitive transcripts: classify source sensitivity and allow source-level exclusion from broad executive views.

Employee privacy: retain organizational decisions and work context, not private life or performance scoring. Do not



Accidental disclosure: answer generator receives only authorized recalled evidence.

Deletion/correction: delete or reprocess source documents where supported; mark canonical decisions 
corrected/superseded; preserve audit records consistent with policy. 
 
Stale memory: show dates, status, and drift; do not present old observations as timeless truth. 
 
Prompt injection in documents: treat source content as evidence, not instructions; extraction prompts explicitly 
ignore embedded commands. 
 
Hallucination/attribution: no historical claim without evidence reference; low-confidence extraction is queued for 
review.

30-Second Pitch 
Companies remember decisions but forget the reasoning around them. DecisionPrint turns PRDs, meetings, 
architecture docs, and postmortems into a living decision memory powered by Hindsight. When a team asks, “Should 
we use Kafka?”, it does not just retrieve an old note saying Kafka was rejected. It remembers that Kafka was rejected 
when there were only two consumers and no replay requirement, compares that with today’s 15 consumers and replay 
needs, flags Decision Drift, and shows the evidence. It also remembers what happened after past decisions, so the 
organization learns instead of repeatedly starting from zero. 
2-Minute Pitch 
Every organization accumulates hundreds of technical and product decisions. The final choice might be written down, 
but the reasoning is fragmented across meetings, PRDs, architecture docs, and postmortems. Months later, “we 
decided not to use Kafka” becomes folklore — the original constraints disappear. 
DecisionPrint is an organizational decision-memory system built around Hindsight. We retain historical and current 
project evidence into a long-term memory bank. Hindsight preserves facts, time, entities, relationships, and evolving 
observations. When a new project lead asks a question, DecisionPrint recalls comparable historical decisions and 
shows the conditions under which they were made. 
Our killer example is Kafka. Project Alpha rejected Kafka because it had two consumers, moderate traffic, and no 
replay. Six months later Project Nova has 15 consumers, high volume, replay, and asynchronous workflows. 
DecisionPrint does not say the old decision was wrong and it does not automatically choose Kafka. It shows that the 
assumptions behind the old decision have drifted and that reconsideration is warranted. 
Then we go one step further. If a later postmortem says a past decision contributed to an incident, DecisionPrint 
connects the decision to the downstream consequence with explicit causal confidence. Over time, Hindsight 
observations and mental models turn individual project evidence into living organizational learning. This is not chat with 
documents. It is memory for how an organization reasons. 
Final Product Definition 
DecisionPrint is a Hindsight-native organizational decision-memory agent that continuously converts 
project evidence into temporal, evidence-backed memory of decisions, reasons, constraints, 
alternatives, outcomes, and evolving organizational beliefs; retrieves that memory when new 
decisions arise; detects when historical premises no longer match current reality; and presents the 
result as a traceable decision brief while leaving final judgment to humans.
